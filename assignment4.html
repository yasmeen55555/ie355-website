Assignment 4

IE 355 — Human Factors Ergonomics

BetterUse Engineering

DFMEA / PFMEA Risk Analysis, REBA and NIOSH Lifting Equation

Engineering Note: Calculations and risk ratings are based on illustrative engineering assumptions.

1. Introduction

BetterUse Engineering is a virtual engineering company focused on improving the safety, comfort, and usability of everyday products. For this assignment, the selected product is a handheld hair dryer.

Traditional handheld hair dryers can create ergonomic challenges, including arm and shoulder fatigue, uncomfortable wrist positions, repetitive movements, and prolonged exposure to noise. These problems may increase physical discomfort during extended use.

The purpose of this assignment is to identify potential product and process failures using Design Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (DFMEA) and Process Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (PFMEA). The project also applies Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA) to evaluate working posture and the NIOSH Lifting Equation to assess manual lifting risks during a manufacturing or packaging task.

The results will be used to recommend engineering improvements that reduce ergonomic risks and improve user safety, product usability, and workplace efficiency.

2. Design Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (DFMEA)

2.1 Purpose of DFMEA

DFMEA is a systematic engineering method used to identify potential product design failures, evaluate their effects, and prioritize improvements before a product is manufactured.

At BetterUse Engineering, DFMEA is applied to the handheld hair dryer to identify ergonomic and safety problems related to product weight, handle design, wrist posture, noise, and stability.

2.2 DFMEA Risk Calculation

RPN = S × O × D

Where:

  • S — Severity: How serious the failure's effects are (1–10).
  • O — Occurrence: How likely the failure is to occur (1–10).
  • D — Detection: How unlikely the failure is to be detected before it affects the user (1–10).

A higher RPN indicates a greater need for investigation and corrective action. Severity must also be considered independently, especially for safety-related failures.

This is the highest RPN in the illustrative DFMEA analysis, so wrist positioning is a priority for design improvement.

2.5 Proposed Engineering Improvements

  1. Lightweight design: Reduce hair dryer mass to decrease arm and shoulder fatigue.
  2. Ergonomic handle: Use a contoured, non-slip handle to improve grip comfort.
  3. Improved wrist positioning: Adjust the handle angle to support a more neutral wrist posture.
  4. Noise reduction: Improve fan and motor design and reduce vibration.
  5. Adjustable stand: Add a stable stand for appropriate hands-free operation.

By prioritizing ergonomic handle geometry, reducing product weight, and improving stability, BetterUse Engineering can develop a safer and more comfortable handheld hair dryer.

The next stage is to evaluate manufacturing and assembly failures using PFMEA.

3. Process Failure Mode and Effects Analysis (PFMEA)

3.1 Purpose of PFMEA

PFMEA identifies possible failures during manufacturing, assembly, inspection, and packaging.

At BetterUse Engineering, PFMEA is applied to ergonomic handheld hair dryer production to identify problems affecting product quality, employee safety, and customer satisfaction.

The analysis focuses on handle assembly, motor installation, electrical wiring, quality inspection, and packaging/material handling.

3.2 PFMEA Risk Calculation

RPN = Severity × Occurrence × Detection = S × O × D

Each factor is rated from 1 to 10. A higher detection rating means a failure is less likely to be detected by existing controls.

  1. Handle assembly: Introduce alignment fixtures and standardized visual instructions.
  2. Motor installation: Use standardized fastening procedures and vibration testing.
  3. Electrical wiring: Identify connectors clearly and conduct electrical safety testing.
  4. Quality inspection: Implement inspection checklists, functional testing, and documented results.

Improved assembly procedures, electrical testing, inspection, and workstation ergonomics can reduce potential failures and improve product quality and employee safety.

4. Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA)

4.1 Purpose of REBA

Rapid Entire Body Assessment (REBA) evaluates working postures associated with musculoskeletal disorder risks.

It considers the neck, trunk, legs, upper arm, lower arm, wrist, external force, grip quality, and activity.

At BetterUse Engineering, REBA is applied to a person using a handheld hair dryer during extended styling.

5. NIOSH Lifting Equation Analysis

5.1 Purpose of the NIOSH Lifting Equation

The Revised NIOSH Lifting Equation (RNLE) evaluates the physical demands of qualifying manual lifting tasks and estimates a recommended weight limit.

At BetterUse Engineering, the equation is applied to a packaging employee lifting cartons of hair dryers from a low storage platform to a packing table.

5.2 Description of the Lifting Task

Task: Lifting cartons from a storage platform onto a packing table.

VariableAssumed Value
Carton weight12 kg
Horizontal distance H40 cm
Initial vertical hand height V50 cm
Vertical travel distance D50 cm
Asymmetry angle A30°
Lifting frequency1 lift/minute
Task duration1 hour
Coupling qualityGood

The task assumes a two-handed lift, stable footing, and suitable environmental conditions.

5.3 NIOSH Recommended Weight Limit Formula

RWL = LC × HM × VM × DM × AM × FM × CM
  • LC = Load Constant = 23 kg
  • HM = Horizontal Multiplier
  • VM = Vertical Multiplier
  • DM = Distance Multiplier
  • AM = Asymmetry Multiplier
  • FM = Frequency Multiplier
  • CM = Coupling Multiplier

5.4 Calculation of Each Multiplier

1. Load Constant

LC = 23 kg

2. Horizontal Multiplier

HM = 25 / H = 25 / 40 = 0.625

3. Vertical Multiplier

VM = 1 − 0.003 × |V − 75|
VM = 1 − 0.003 × |50 − 75| = 0.925

4. Distance Multiplier

DM = 0.82 + 4.5 / D
DM = 0.82 + 4.5 / 50 = 0.91

5. Asymmetry Multiplier

AM = 1 − 0.0032 × A
AM = 1 − 0.0032 × 30 = 0.904

6. Frequency Multiplier

For the assumed lifting frequency and duration, the working example uses:

FM = 0.94

7. Coupling Multiplier

CM = 1.00

5.5 Recommended Weight Limit Calculation

RWL = 23 × 0.625 × 0.925 × 0.91 × 0.904 × 0.94 × 1.00
RWL ≈ 10.28 kg

This is the illustrative recommended weight limit calculated for the origin of the lift.

5.6 Lifting Index Calculation

LI = Actual Load Weight / RWL
LI = 12 / 10.28 ≈ 1.17

5.7 Interpretation of Results

Lifting IndexGeneral Interpretation
LI ≤ 1.0Within the calculated recommended weight limit
LI > 1.0Increased concern; improvements should be considered
LI > 3.0Substantially elevated lifting demands

Result: LI ≈ 1.17.

The carton is heavier than the recommended weight limit under the assumed conditions, suggesting a need to improve the lifting task.

5.8 Proposed Ergonomic Improvements

  1. Reduce carton weight from 12 kg to approximately 8 kg.
  2. Reduce horizontal reach from 40 cm to 25 cm.
  3. Increase initial lifting height from 50 cm to 75 cm.
  4. Reduce vertical travel using adjustable platforms.
  5. Eliminate unnecessary twisting.
  6. Provide suitable handholds and mechanical lifting assistance when needed.

5.11 Limitations of the Analysis

The calculations use hypothetical measurements. A real assessment requires direct measurements, verified frequency and coupling multipliers, and analysis of lifting geometry at both the origin and destination.

The RNLE does not apply to every type of manual handling, such as one-handed lifting, carrying, pushing, or pulling.

The more restrictive lifting condition should determine the final recommended weight limit.

5.12 Conclusion

The NIOSH analysis indicates that the original packaging task may create unnecessary lower-back loading due to carton weight, horizontal reach, and twisting.

Reducing carton weight, improving workstation height, eliminating twisting, and moving materials closer to the worker can improve ergonomic conditions.

The illustrative Lifting Index decreases from 1.17 to 0.37, subject to verification of the full lifting task.

6. Overall Results and Engineering Recommendations

6.1 Summary of Findings

Four complementary methods were applied to BetterUse Engineering's hair dryer and manufacturing and packaging operations.

6.2 Prioritized Engineering Actions

  1. Electrical safety: Implement connector identification, wiring checks, and electrical safety testing. The wiring failure has a severity rating of 10.
  2. Inspection and process control: Standardize inspection criteria, record test results, and improve defect feedback.
  3. User ergonomics: Reduce hair dryer weight, improve handle geometry, and provide an adjustable stand.
  4. Packaging ergonomics: Reduce carton weight, improve reach distances, minimize vertical travel, and eliminate twisting.

6.3 Repeatable Engineering Risk-Assessment Procedure

  1. Define the product or task, intended users, and potential failure modes.
  2. Record assumptions and measure relevant inputs such as weight, angles, lifting distances, frequency, and duration.
  3. Assign severity, occurrence, and detection ratings. Calculate RPN = S × O × D and separately flag high-severity safety hazards.
  4. For posture-related tasks, apply REBA and document Group A, Group B, Table C, and activity adjustments.
  5. For qualifying two-handed lifts, calculate NIOSH multipliers at the origin and destination. Use the limiting RWL to calculate the Lifting Index.
  6. Select engineering controls and establish measurable improvement targets.
  7. Repeat the assessment after changes and document the remaining risks.

6.4 Interpretation and Validation

The proposed RPN changes are scenario estimates, not demonstrated changes in failure rates. REBA scores are ordinal screening values, and NIOSH lifting indices indicate relative task demands rather than individual injury probability.

Before implementation, verify REBA table lookups, NIOSH frequency and coupling multipliers, lifting geometry at the origin and destination, and actual workplace observations.

7. Final Conclusion

This project demonstrates how industrial engineering connects product design, manufacturing quality, and worker ergonomics.

The DFMEA identifies wrist positioning, excessive product weight, and lack of stable hands-free support as design concerns. The PFMEA identifies quality inspection and high-severity electrical wiring hazards as priorities for process controls.

The REBA example highlights raised-arm and wrist postures during styling. The NIOSH example highlights reaching, twisting, and carton weight during packaging.

BetterUse Engineering should prioritize electrical safety, improve hair dryer handle and support options, standardize inspections, and redesign material-handling stations.

The numerical before-and-after estimates provide a foundation for engineering planning but require physical testing, observation, and completed assessment worksheets before being reported as actual risk reductions.
